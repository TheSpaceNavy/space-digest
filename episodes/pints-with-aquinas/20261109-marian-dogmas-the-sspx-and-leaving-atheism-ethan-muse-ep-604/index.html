Marian Dogmas, The SSPX, and Leaving Atheism (Ethan Muse) | Ep. 604

Pints With Aquinas · 9 November 2026 · 3h 12m · written up 9 November at 22:50

Miracles, Marian doctrine, conversion, the SSPX, and Mormon origins are tied together by one test: authority.

  • Credible miracles are rare because serious investigation must rule out fraud and establish several lines of evidence. 7:14
  • Catholics should be cautious with miracle claims and keep Jesus and the Church’s core teaching at the center of faith. 29:21
  • Mary’s unique role is defended through her motherhood of Christ, her place as the new Eve, and her participation in his suffering. 59:18
  • The speaker describes moving from Protestantism to atheism, then through idealism and religious pluralism toward Christianity. 1:31:34
  • He chose Catholicism over other apostolic churches because it showed stronger unity, continuity, and authority across history. 1:52:17
  • He rejects the SSPX’s parallel-church position and says modern Catholic teaching on capital punishment can be understood as a prudential judgment. 2:09:41
  • Joseph Smith’s treasure-hunting practices and seer stones are presented as continuous with his later account of translating the Book of Mormon. 2:58:23

“Fitting gets you to plausibility. Um it doesn't necessarily get you to probability.” [1:08:00]

Worth your time if you want a candid Catholic case for authority, Marian belief, conversion, and staying loyal during church crises.

Most surprising

  1. “Really really credible miracles are rare.” 7:44Readers might expect miracle claims to be plentiful and persuasive.
  2. “God doesn't love everybody equally.” 1:25:44Many Christians would assume equal divine love is the safer claim.
  3. “I’m going to crucify my intellect” and conform myself to the mind of the church. 2:03:25The speaker describes submission to church teaching as a dramatic intellectual reversal.
  4. “the translation method for the Book of Mormon is literally exactly the same sear stones that he used to hunt for buried treasure” 2:58:23Readers might expect a distinct, explicitly religious translation method.

Yes, Protestants Can Pray the Rosary. Here's How.

Pints With Aquinas · 7 October 2026 · 14 min · written up 8 October at 00:48

A Catholic guide says Protestants can pray the Rosary while setting aside several Catholic beliefs.

  • The Rosary developed as a way for people who could not read or afford a Bible to replace the monks’ 150 daily Psalms with 150 Hail Marys. 1:44
  • The five main Protestant objections are the beads, repeated prayer, calling Mary “Mother of God,” asking saints to pray, and two Marian mysteries. 2:28
  • The beads are not magical; they simply fit humans as physical beings, much like a beautiful printed Bible can feel better than an app. 3:34
  • Repeated prayer is not automatically empty: Scripture repeats prayers, and the Rosary uses repetition to support meditation on Christ’s life, death, and resurrection. 5:05
  • Protestants can omit the later part of the Hail Mary, which was added during the Black Plague, while keeping words drawn from Scripture. 7:10
  • They can also skip the Assumption and Coronation mysteries and still pray the Rosary without adopting Catholic beliefs. 9:56

“Repetition in and of itself is not vain.” [6:08]

Worth your time if you are curious whether prayer practices can cross Protestant-Catholic lines.

Most surprising

  1. The Rosary began partly as a substitute for the 150 Psalms for people who could not read or afford a Bible. 1:44Many assume the Rosary began mainly as a formal Catholic devotion.
  2. “You could simply pray, Hail Mary, full of grace, the Lord is with thee.” 7:50Many assume every line of the modern Hail Mary is required.

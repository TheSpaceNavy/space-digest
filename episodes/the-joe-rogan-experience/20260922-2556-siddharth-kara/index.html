#2556 - Siddharth Kara

The Joe Rogan Experience · 22 September 2026 · 2h 22m · written up 22 September at 23:24

The clean-tech race is tied to slavery, while past abolition shows how change can still win.

  • Mining for cobalt and other battery metals has brought child labor, slavery, militia violence and severe environmental damage to the Congo. 1:25
  • About 300,000 people dig for cobalt each day, including roughly 30,000 children, and much of the work is described as slavery. 11:56
  • Cobalt dug by children and militias still reaches phones, electric vehicles and tablets through formal supply chains. 35:27
  • Sugar made Jamaica’s exports worth five times those of all thirteen American colonies combined, powering the growth of slavery. 57:10
  • The Zorg’s crew threw about 130 to 140 Africans overboard to preserve drinking water, prompting a legal fight over whether enslaved people were cargo. 1:16:10
  • Britain paid slave owners £20 million for abolition, while taxpayers continued repaying the loan until 2015. 1:43:00

“Even if you didn't need all these metals ... tomorrow, you cannot just leave behind an apocalypse.” [2:17:24]

Worth your time if you want the hidden human cost behind technology and the history of successful moral campaigns.

Most surprising

  1. “On any given day there are at least 300,000 people men and children digging for cobalt with their bare hands and pickaxes and shovels.” 11:56Readers might expect artisanal mining to involve far fewer people.
  2. “They throw 130 to 140 Africans overboard into the sea to drown.” 1:16:10A reader might expect deaths from disease, not deliberate mass killing to conserve water.
  3. Britain’s compensation loan to slave owners was still being repaid by taxpayers in 2015. 1:43:30Readers likely assumed slavery’s financial consequences ended soon after abolition.
  4. “Almost 10% are kids,” meaning roughly 30,000 children work in the cobalt mines daily. 12:24The scale of child labor is much larger than a few isolated cases.
  5. “The rivers and streams and wellwater around the villages came back in the fours and threes which is called hyper acidic.” 2:19:18A reader might assume polluted water is harmful, not dangerously acidic.

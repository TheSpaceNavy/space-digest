The $100B Niches Hiding Inside Payments

The a16z Show · 3 September 2026 · 1h 0m · Reached back for, from the archive · written up 12 September at 18:59

Payments look crowded, but the biggest opportunities may hide in problems people barely recognize as payments.

  • Apple Pay and Google Pay took off because new liability rules, contactless terminals, COVID, and smartphones arrived together. 3:20
  • Even secure phone technology could not remove the card networks’ roughly two-and-a-half-second limit for in-person payments. 4:23
  • The speakers argue that payment niches are rarely small: even narrow opportunities can reach $100 billion, while bigger transfers often earn less. 6:04
  • Affirm found its breakthrough when Beautylish showed installment payments before checkout, lifting purchases by 30 percent immediately. 33:13
  • Affirm later shifted from helping people afford purchases to helping merchants create demand and reach customers. 43:07

“Convenience just trumps everything else as the total amount you're trying to send goes down.” — Max Levchin [17:23]

Worth your time if you want to understand why payment companies win by changing behavior, not just moving money.

Most surprising

  1. “There are no niches and payments that are smaller than $100 billion.” — Max Levchin6:04Most industries appear to contain many small, specialized markets.
  2. Some for-profit education institutions paid merchant discount rates as high as 50%. 35:15Readers would expect payment fees to be only a few percentage points.
  3. Affirm has “negative customer acquisition cost” because merchants pay it to acquire customers. 44:40Consumer companies usually pay advertising platforms to find customers.
  4. Grocery shopping is already “100% agentic” through human shoppers who substitute products for customers. 58:03Agentic shopping sounds like something software must do.

How the Midterm Results Change AI

The AI Daily Brief · 4 November 2026 · 25 min · written up 4 November at 20:00

The election did not stop AI, but it made data centers a political liability that developers can no longer ignore.

  • AI itself ranked below the economy, healthcare, democracy, and education, while 61% of voters disliked candidates who supported local data centers. 2:20
  • Democrats won key races by tying data centers to high living costs, tech wealth, corruption, and communities paying for private infrastructure. 4:06
  • Texas became a major warning sign for the industry after Democrats took power and inherited more than 470 gigawatts of proposed data centers. 9:25
  • The 41 data-center proposals on state and local ballots all appeared headed for passage, signaling that AI companies had lost the public argument. 17:16
  • Washington is more likely to pursue narrower rules—such as whistleblower protection, incident reporting, and stronger legal responsibility—than a sweeping AI law. 18:19
  • The likely compromise is tougher community rules, including no secret agreements, developers paying their own costs, and local residents having a real say. 21:57

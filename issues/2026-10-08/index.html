The Best Way to Test New AI Models

The AI Daily Brief · 7 October 2026 · 50 min · written up 8 October at 00:49

The best AI model is the one that wins on your real work, not a public leaderboard.

  • Public benchmarks can mislead because models may have trained on them, while real-world value depends on the task. 2:34
  • A personal benchmark should use representative work, compare models or tools, and include at least one task previous systems failed. 4:18
  • Blind, anonymous testing matters because people tend to favor familiar tools and may recognize or excuse their weaknesses. 6:05
  • Testing seven options across six tasks became too tedious, showing that a useful comparison must stay manageable. 14:34
  • Identical prompts and settings can still produce different results, so one successful or poor run is not enough evidence. 21:01
  • The final choice should weigh quality, cost, speed, features, and habit—and may mean switching, splitting tasks, or staying put. 25:28
  • In one test, personal ratings favored Fable and GPT Sol equally, while the more expensive option offered no clear practical win. 36:54
  • The speaker concludes that open-source models may now handle most knowledge work when paired with a strong supporting tool. 46:01

“Even if we're using the exact same model with the exact same configuration and the exact same prompt, you will get different results.” [21:01]

Worth your time if you want a practical way to choose AI tools for your own work.

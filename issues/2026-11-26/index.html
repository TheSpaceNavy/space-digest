Replay: On Food as Medicine (2020)

The a16z Show · 26 November 2026 · 19 min · written up 26 November at 16:03

Food can act like medicine, but only when healthy choices are affordable, easy, and built into daily life.

  • Food shares key advantages with medicine: it is widely used, generally accessible, and people must take it every day. 0:34
  • Medically tailored meals and food vouchers have improved health and saved money, but delivery remains a major cost barrier. 1:43
  • Food insecurity sharply raises household healthcare costs, from 15% with mild insecurity to as much as 70% with severe insecurity. 5:26
  • Healthier food can be a cheaper treatment for chronic illness: one program cut costs for very sick diabetes patients by two-thirds. 10:38
  • Sustainable diets should allow occasional treats, since banning birthday cake or pumpkin pie is unlikely to work long term. 13:26
  • The biggest opportunity may be reducing friction—making the healthy choice the easy, affordable choice—rather than trying to force behavior change. 17:23

“It's the ease of use and the access and the cost that really the sort of axes of that decision that have to get improved.” [17:23]

Worth your time if you care about healthcare costs, food access, and practical ways to prevent chronic disease.

Most surprising

  1. Severe food insecurity can raise a household’s annual healthcare costs by as much as 70%. 5:26Most people would expect food hardship to affect grocery bills, not medical spending.
  2. A healthy-food program cut healthcare costs for very sick diabetes patients by two-thirds. 10:38Diet changes are often treated as advice, not as a major cost-saving treatment.
  3. “We all do the easy thing.” 17:23The problem may be access and friction, not a lack of awareness or motivation.

How Anthropic Went From Anti-Product to $81B a Year

The AI Daily Brief · 26 November 2026 · 19 min · written up 26 November at 16:03

Anthropic became an AI leader by giving up its plan to avoid products and betting early on coding agents.

  • Anthropic began as a safety-first research lab that considered becoming a nonprofit and planned to stay under 50 people. 1:35
  • Its founders avoided commercial plans, but relied on large donations and a $500 million investment from Sam Bankman-Fried. 2:16

  • Claude 3.5 Sonnet pulled ahead in coding, winning over developers even though Claude had only a fraction of ChatGPT’s consumer users. 8:22

“It's sad that we have to make this trade-off, but it's going to be worth it.” [7:13]

Worth your time if you want to understand why coding, not chat, became AI’s biggest business opportunity.

Most surprising

  2. Anthropic’s founders seriously considered becoming a nonprofit and planned a lab of no more than about 50 people. 1:35Today’s multibillion-dollar AI company began with an intentionally tiny, research-only plan.

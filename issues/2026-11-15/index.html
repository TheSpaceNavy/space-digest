7 Things AI-Native Companies Do Differently

The AI Daily Brief · 15 November 2026 · 23 min · written up 15 November at 13:53

The best AI-native companies are replacing fixed digital teammates with context-rich systems that summon the right workers for each task.

  • Advanced companies give agents access to broad company context, including meetings, messages, documents, tools, and decision records. 3:55
  • AI-native work spreads beyond engineering, with agents handling marketing, support, finance, legal, partnerships, and other functions. 6:02
  • Instead of waiting for prompts, agents begin work when signals such as customer messages, renewals, invoices, or alerts appear. 7:14
  • Organizations increasingly use one orchestrator to assign fresh subagents narrow tasks, rather than maintaining a large roster of permanent agents. 8:21
  • AI-native systems require agents to verify one another’s work with tests, evidence, adversarial reviews, and production checks. 9:23
  • Companies match human review to risk while keeping a named person responsible for every outcome. 13:09

“There's a persistent bundle of context that I can drop any set of agents into at any given time.” [19:04]

Worth your time if you want practical patterns for moving from AI experiments to company-wide systems.

Most surprising

  1. Uber’s library contains 3,600 skills and runs more than 30,000 times a day. 14:50Most people picture AI use as isolated chats, not a shared operating system.
  2. “Splitting agents by job function, like planner, coder, test or reviewer, tends to backfire.” 17:32Traditional team structures seem like the obvious blueprint for AI workers.
  3. Snap puts more than half of its agent investment into guardrails rather than the agents themselves. 14:16Most investment would seem likely to favor smarter models and more capable agents.

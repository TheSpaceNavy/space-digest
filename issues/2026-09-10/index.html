How AI Is Rewriting the Power Law of Venture Capital

The a16z Show · 10 September 2026 · 49 min

AI is making venture returns more extreme, while even the biggest software winners face pressure from faster-moving rivals.

  • AI reached $100 billion in revenue in four years, compared with 15 years for SaaS, while reaching into much broader economic activity. 4:45
  • Fewer than 1% of 3,000 U.S. venture firms have produced consistent three-times net returns over two decades. 11:37
  • Reasoning models turned Harvey from a weak-looking product into something lawyers actively wanted, easing fears about AI hallucinations. 24:17
  • Investors argue that venture funds should be concentrated because spreading bets too widely usually produces average returns. 28:28
  • AI is changing private equity, where buyers now favor AI-native firms or older software companies whose growth speeds up through AI. 30:40
  • AI spending remains highly uneven: the median U.S. company spends $12 per employee monthly, versus $7,000 for the top 1%. 36:29

“Even the morning of the acquisition announcement people were still saying that Cursor is dead.” [22:40]

Worth your time if you want to understand why AI could widen the gap between venture winners and everyone else.
